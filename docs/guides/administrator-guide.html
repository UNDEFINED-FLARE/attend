1How UniAttend works

Lecturers open attendance for a class; students check in on their phones by scanning a live QR code in the venue; the system checks the evidence and records the result. You set it up and keep it running.

A check-in, from start to finish

  1. The lecturer opens attendance for a class, from the timetable or from the module page. A QR code that changes every few seconds appears on the projector, with a short join code.
  2. Students scan it in the UniAttend app or website. Their phone sends the scan, its location and a device check.
  3. UniAttend weighs the evidence: the live QR, whether the phone is inside the venue (and how accurate the location is), and whether the same phone already checked in someone else.
  4. Strong evidence is accepted as present (or late, after the grace period). Weak evidence goes to review — it is never turned into an absence automatically.
  5. When the lecturer closes the class, students who never tried are recorded absent; students who tried and were refused go to review.

2Register your institution

One person registers the institution and becomes its first administrator. You can set everything up while you wait for approval.

  1. Create an account on UniAttend with your work email and confirm the email UniAttend sends you.
  2. On the Get set up screen choose Register an institution. Enter the institution's name, a short name (used in its web address) and a contact email.
  3. Wait for approval. UniAttend checks the registration and approves it. Until then you can build structure, people and the timetable; only attendance sessions wait for approval.
  4. Your institution starts on a 30-day trial. The dashboard tells you when a trial or subscription is ending.
TipAdd a second administrator early (chapter 9), so the institution is never locked out if one person leaves.

Lecturers and students join by email

You never create passwords for anyone. You add lecturers and students to UniAttend's register with their email or student number (chapter 5). When they sign up with that email and confirm it, UniAttend links their account to their record automatically and they see their modules.

4Faculties, departments and venues

Open Venues & map (the Faculties & venues page). Structure decides who sees what; venues decide where a check-in counts.

Faculties and departments

  1. Under Faculties, add each faculty with a name and short code.
  2. Under Departments, add each department, choosing its faculty.

Lecture venues

A check-in's location is compared with the venue. A venue can be a point with a radius, or an outline of the building.

  1. Use Campus map & lecture venues to add a venue and place it on the map.
  2. For a point, use a radius of about half the building's width plus 15 m.
  3. For large or irregular halls, draw the outline instead — or use Outline every venue automatically, which proposes outlines from the map for you to check and apply.
TipIndoors, phone location is often accurate to only 20–60 m. A check-in slightly outside a venue goes to review, not to absent, so a generous outline is better than a tight one.

5Modules, lecturers and students

You can add records by hand, or bring them in from files and your own systems (chapter 6). Both lead to the same register.

Modules

  1. Open Modules and use Add a module: code, title, department, and Required attendance (%) (leave it blank to use the institution default).
  2. Use Assign a lecturer: choose the module, the lecturer and the role (Primary or Assistant).
  3. Use Enrol or remove a student for single changes; bulk enrolment is easier by import.

You can also upload a list of lecturer assignments (PDF, Word, Excel or CSV); UniAttend shows the matches for you to confirm.

Lecturers

Open People. Under Add a lecturer enter the staff number, full name, the email they will sign up with, and the department. They get lecturer access as soon as they sign up and confirm that email.

Students

Under Add students, add one at a time, paste a list, or upload a CSV with these columns:

student_number, full_name, email, module_code

  • Use one row per student per module to enrol a student in several modules.
  • Importing the same file again is safe: nothing is duplicated.
  • Rows with problems are skipped and listed by row number.

Groups

Students can be placed in groups within a module (for example tutorial groups). A class for one group notifies only that group.

Records are archived, not erased Removing a student or lecturer who has attendance history archives the record; their history is kept. Records are deleted only under your retention period (chapter 13).

6Bringing data in: imports and connections

Open Integrations. You can upload files whenever you like, or connect UniAttend to your systems so data stays up to date without typing it twice.

Import a file (Import Centre)

  1. Choose Import data → New import and upload a CSV or Excel file.
  2. Match the columns once: tell UniAttend which column is the student number, the name, the module code, and so on. Save the matching to reuse it next time.
  3. Preview. UniAttend shows exactly what will be added, updated, left unchanged or rejected, and why — before anything changes.
  4. Confirm. The import is applied in one step: all of it or none of it.
  5. Download the rejected rows as CSV, fix them, and import them again.

You can import faculties, departments, programmes, academic periods, venues, modules, lecturers, students, enrolments, lecturer assignments and dated timetable classes.

Connect a system (Integration Centre)

MethodHow it worksYou need

Start with Connect, choose your system (ITS, Banner, PeopleSoft, Moodle, CELCAT…) and follow the wizard. Systems without a direct connector use one of the three methods above with their own exports.

Keeping an eye on it

Sync activity lists every sync and import: what came in, what changed, what was rejected and why. You can try a failed run again.

How UniAttend protects your register
  • Nothing is deleted because it is missing from an incomplete import.
  • Enrolments are dropped only when a source says its roster is complete and the connection allows it — and only enrolments that connection created. Attendance history is always kept.
  • If a "complete" roster would suddenly drop most students, nothing is dropped and the count is held back for you to check.
  • Students are matched by student number, so a changed email updates the same student.

Preparing your first import

  1. Import in this order: faculties and departments, then modules, then lecturers, then students with their enrolments, then lecturer assignments, then the timetable. Each step can then find what it refers to.
  2. Use the same module codes everywhere (for example CSC1541). A row naming a module UniAttend doesn't know is listed, not guessed.
  3. Check student numbers against your student number format (chapter 8) before you upload.
  4. Start small: import one department first, check it in Data, then import the rest.

A student and enrolment file can look like this (one row per student per module):

7The timetable and dated classes

Open Timetable. Classes added here appear on every enrolled student's timetable and campus map, and become the classes lecturers open attendance for.

Adding the weekly timetable

  • Import from your timetable source — a timetable link set up for your institution.
  • Import a timetable PDF — UniAttend reads the PDF and shows the classes it found for you to confirm.
  • Add a class by hand: module, day, start and end time, venue, type (lecture, tutorial, practical, test…), and an optional group.

Dated classes

Every night UniAttend turns the weekly timetable into dated classes for the next two weeks. To do it now, press Prepare the next two weeks now under Classes and attendance.

  • Choose which kinds of class take attendance (lectures, tutorials, practicals by default; tests and other events not). A lecturer can still take attendance at any class.
  • Lecturers can cancel, move, change the venue of, or hand a class to a substitute; the students in that class (or group) are told. A change made by hand is never overwritten by the nightly refresh.
  • A lecturer presses Open attendance on a class from 30 minutes before it starts. Pressing it twice returns the same session — never a duplicate.
TipThe timetable never opens attendance on its own and never marks anyone present. A person always opens it.

Attendance statuses

Besides present, late, absent and excused you can add your own statuses (for example "Left early"), each counting as present, late, absent or excused.

Set the pattern your student numbers follow (for example eight digits: ^[0-9]{8}$) so imports catch typing mistakes.

Before go-liveReview these settings with your faculty or academic office, then sign off Attendance policy on the go-live checklist.

  2. Under Give role, enter their email and choose the role. For a faculty or department administrator, also choose the faculty or department.

10Design your platform

11Day to day: dashboard, reviews, early warning

Dashboard

Today's present, absent and late figures, the attendance rate, attendance by department, recent check-ins and the number of check-ins waiting for review. Use Send an announcement to message everyone, a faculty, a department, a module, or one person.

Reviews

Open Reviews. Check-ins with weak evidence wait here for a person; they never become absences on their own. Each case says why it is there and shows its evidence:

ReasonMeaning
Outside the venueThe phone appeared to be outside the venue, with its distance and accuracy.
Imprecise or no locationLocation was too vague, or the student did not share it.
Device used by another studentThe same phone checked in someone else in this class.
Typed codeThe student typed the code instead of scanning the live QR.
Checked in without signalThe phone was offline; the check-in was sent later with its original time.
Tried, but was refusedFor example the QR had expired; the student is in review instead of absent.
Late arrivalArrived after the grace period, awaiting the lecturer.
  1. Filter by reason if you like, and tick one student, a whole class, or many.
  2. Choose Present, Late, Absent or Excused. A reason is required for absent or excused.
  3. The student is told; the decision, who made it and why are kept and listed under Decided in the last 30 days.

Classes without a register lists timetabled classes that ended without anyone taking attendance — useful to follow up with lecturers.

Disputes

Students can dispute an absent, late or pending record. The module's lecturers (or an attendance officer) uphold or reject it; the student is told the outcome.

Early warning

Open Early warning. Alerts open by themselves when a student's attendance falls below the requirement or they miss several classes in a row. Set Judge attendance after (classes) and Missed in a row (classes), then record contact and close alerts as students are followed up.

12Reports and exports

Open Reports. Choose a period and, if you like, a faculty, department or module, then press Show.

  • Headline figures: attendance rate, classes, late arrivals, unresolved records, classes without a register, open disputes.
  • Attendance by faculty, by department and by module, lowest first; a weekly trend.
  • Students at risk: below their module's requirement (counting records still in review as missed) or absent three or more classes in a row.

Only closed classes count. Excused classes are left out, and records in review or disputed are shown separately, never as absences.

Downloads

CSVThe module table or the students-at-risk list, for other systems.
PDFThe report on your letterhead (logo, title, period, page numbers). Use Save as PDF in the print window.

13Privacy, POPIA and your data

Personal information requests

Students and staff can ask to see, correct or delete their information, or object to how it is used. Requests arrive under Policy → Personal information requests with a 30-day clock. Your institution decides; record the outcome so it is kept. Students can also download all their own data themselves.

Keeping records

Set Keep records for (days) (the default is three years). Under Keeping records (POPIA) you can preview what is older than that and delete it, or switch on automatic nightly clean-up. It is off by default: your institution decides when records are deleted. Lecturers removed earlier are anonymised rather than deleted, so past classes still show who ran them.

The audit log

Audit log records role changes, corrections, review decisions, imports, downloads, sign-offs and session events. Nobody can edit it.

Your data

Open Your data:

ActionWhat happens

Biometric dataThe optional fingerprint or face lock on the phone app is checked only on the phone. UniAttend never receives or stores biometric data. Check-ins keep only the distance from the venue and its accuracy, not the student's coordinates.
